Physiotherapy vs chiropractic: which is right for you?

Different training, different core technique, some real overlap. Here's how to tell them apart.

Physiotherapy is built on a broad assessment of movement, strength and posture, using manual therapy, exercise and education. Chiropractic care traditionally centres on spinal adjustment as its primary technique. Both can ease back and neck pain. They get there differently, and one may suit your specific situation better than the other.

How each one actually works

Physiotherapy is a university-trained, science-based practice built on how your joints, muscles and nerves work together. A session typically combines manual therapy, tailored exercise and posture correction, addressing the mechanics behind pain and movement problems so the same issue is less likely to return. The training covers the whole body, not just the spine.

Chiropractic care is also a distinct, university-trained profession, but its central technique is spinal adjustment: a controlled, sudden force applied to a joint, most often in the spine, to improve movement and reduce pain. Some chiropractors also use soft tissue work and rehabilitation exercise, but adjustment and manipulation remain the profession's defining technique.

What a physiotherapy assessment covers

  1. A whole-body assessment

    Checking movement, strength and history, not just the painful joint in isolation.

  2. An honest recommendation

    Manual therapy, exercise, or both, based on what's actually driving your symptoms.

  3. A plan you can take home

    Exercises and self-management so you're not dependent on ongoing passive treatment.

A note on safety

Physiotherapy is adjusted to your comfort throughout, and treatment stops or changes course if something isn't right for you. Joint mobilisation and manipulation, when used, are applied within a broader assessment of your movement and history.

Spinal manipulation of any kind, whichever profession performs it, carries a small, well-documented risk when applied to the neck, most notably to the arteries supplying the brain. This risk is rare, but it's why manipulation should always follow a proper assessment and never be treated as routine or automatic for every neck complaint.

Neither physiotherapy nor chiropractic care is a substitute for urgent medical care. Pain with unexplained weight loss, fever, numbness in the saddle area, or loss of bowel or bladder control needs medical assessment first.

What's the difference between physiotherapy and chiropractic care?

Physiotherapy is built on a broader assessment of movement, strength and posture, using manual therapy, exercise prescription and education to treat the whole musculoskeletal system. Chiropractic care traditionally focuses more narrowly on the spine, using spinal adjustment and manipulation as its primary technique. Physiotherapy generally puts more emphasis on active rehabilitation you do yourself between visits.

Does physiotherapy involve spinal manipulation like chiropractic?

Physiotherapy can include joint mobilisation and, less commonly, manipulation, but it's one tool among many rather than the central technique. The emphasis is on assessing why a joint is restricted in the first place, weak muscles, poor movement patterns, and correcting that alongside any hands-on work.

Which is better for lower back pain?

Both can provide relief for lower back pain, and evidence supports manual therapy from either profession for short-term symptom relief. Physiotherapy's broader evidence base and focus on addressing the underlying movement or strength deficit tends to support longer-term outcomes, particularly when back pain is linked to posture, weakness or an old injury rather than a single acute event.

Is chiropractic treatment evidence-based?

Spinal manipulation, the core chiropractic technique, has reasonable evidence for short-term relief of certain types of back and neck pain. Physiotherapy has a broader and longer-standing evidence base across a wider range of musculoskeletal conditions, since it draws on multiple techniques rather than one primary one. We're upfront about the difference so you can make an informed choice.

Do you offer chiropractic care at Holistic Physio?

No, we're a physiotherapy and acupuncture clinic and don't offer chiropractic care. This page is here to help you understand the difference and decide what fits your situation, even if that means seeking chiropractic care elsewhere.

Do I need a doctor's referral for physiotherapy?

No, you can book directly. A referral is only required for WorkCover, Veterans' Affairs, CTP or a Medicare chronic condition management plan.

Can I claim physiotherapy on my private health fund?

In most cases, yes. The clinic is registered with all major health funds for physiotherapy, so most policies allow you to claim on the spot through HICAPS and just pay the gap. Check with your fund to confirm your level of cover.
