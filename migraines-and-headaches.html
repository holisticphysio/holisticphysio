Headaches that start in the neck

Many headaches don't actually start in the head. They start in the neck. The joints and muscles at the top of your neck, right at the base of your skull, can send pain signals forward along the same nerve pathways that reach your forehead and the area behind your eyes. That's why a headache can feel like it's sitting right behind your eyes when the real problem is a stiff joint or a tight, sore spot lower down in your neck.

In Traditional Chinese Medicine, this pattern is often called Liver Yang Rising. Think of it like pressure building up because built-up tension and stress don't have anywhere to go, so they rise upward instead of settling down. This often shows up as tightness through the temples, jaw and the base of the skull. A physiotherapy assessment finds exactly which joints and muscles in your neck are stiff or tight, then treats them directly. Acupuncture works alongside this by calming your nervous system, so that built-up pressure has somewhere to go instead of building up again.

Good questions

If scans have come back clear but the headaches haven't stopped, you're not alone. Here's what to know.

My scans came back clear, can you still help?

Yes. Many headaches trace back to neck tension, posture or hormonal patterns that don't show up on a scan but respond well to treatment.

Will Acupuncture trigger a migraine?

It's rare. Most people find Acupuncture calming rather than triggering, and treatment is adjusted if you're sensitive to stimulation.

How many sessions before headaches reduce?

Many people notice fewer or milder headaches within 4 to 6 sessions, though this varies with how long the pattern has been established.

Do I need a doctor's referral?

No, you can book directly. A referral is only required for WorkCover, Veterans' Affairs, CTP or a Medicare chronic condition management plan.

Can I claim on my private health fund?

In most cases, yes. The clinic is registered with all major health funds for physiotherapy, Acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine, so most policies allow you to claim on the spot through HICAPS and just pay the gap. Check with your fund to confirm your level of cover.

How do I know if a headache needs urgent medical care rather than physio?

A sudden, severe headache unlike any you've had before, or one with fever, neck stiffness, vision loss, weakness or slurred speech, needs urgent medical assessment, not physiotherapy. For the more common recurring patterns, tension headaches, neck-related headaches or hormonal migraines, physiotherapy and Acupuncture are a good place to start.
