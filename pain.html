Pain doesn't only affect your body, it impacts your mood, energy, sleep and daily life. Instead of just masking the symptoms, we aim to find the root cause and treat it with a holistic mix of therapies.

What influences pain

  • Tissue damage or injury: sprains, strains, fractures
  • Inflammation: helpful for healing, harmful in excess
  • Nerves becoming extra sensitive, common in long-term pain
  • Posture and the way you move, from long hours sitting
  • Muscle tension, often worsened by stress
  • Hormonal changes and lifestyle factors

Short-term vs long-term pain

Short-term pain (doctors call this acute pain) is your body's alarm system after an injury or sudden illness. It usually settles as the tissue heals. Long-term pain (doctors call this chronic pain) is different. It lasts more than three months, sometimes even after the original injury has fully healed, because the nervous system itself has become more sensitive to signals it used to ignore. The brain and nerves have essentially learned the pain pathway too well, so the alarm keeps going off even once there's nothing new to alarm about.

In Traditional Chinese Medicine, this pattern of pain that lingers is often described as Qi and Blood Stasis, where the body's energy and circulation have become stuck rather than flowing freely through the area. Acupuncture and hands-on therapy both work to get that flow moving again, easing pain while also settling the nervous system's overreaction, rather than just numbing the area for a while.

  2. Physiotherapy

    Assesses movement and posture, using hands-on therapy and targeted exercise.

  3. Clinical Pilates

    Strengthens the muscles that support the spine and joints, preventing recurrence.

Will treatment make my pain worse before it gets better?

We work within your comfort level throughout. Some mild soreness after hands-on treatment is normal, but we won't push you into unnecessary pain.

I've tried other treatments that didn't work, will this be different?

Combining Physiotherapy, Acupuncture and Chinese Herbal Medicine lets us address the cause from multiple angles, not just repeat what hasn't worked.

How long until I feel some relief?

Many people notice a change within the first few sessions, though chronic pain often needs a longer, layered approach. We'll be upfront about what to expect.

Do I need a doctor's referral?

No, you can book directly. A referral is only required for WorkCover, Veterans' Affairs, CTP or a Medicare chronic condition management plan.

Can I claim on my private health fund?

In most cases, yes. The clinic is registered with all major health funds for Physiotherapy, Acupuncture and Chinese Herbal Medicine, so most policies allow you to claim on the spot through HICAPS and just pay the gap. Check with your fund to confirm your level of cover.

When should pain be checked by a doctor first?

Pain with unexplained weight loss, fever, numbness in the saddle area, or loss of bowel or bladder control needs urgent medical assessment, not Physiotherapy. For the far more common everyday aches, muscle pain and long-standing pain patterns, Physiotherapy and Acupuncture are appropriate first-line care.
